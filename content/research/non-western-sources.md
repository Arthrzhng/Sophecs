# Sophecs Research Brief

## Editorial cautions

- **Confucianism:** "Confucian role ethics" is a contemporary reconstruction, especially associated with Roger Ames and Henry Rosemont; other scholars classify Confucian thought as virtue ethics. The *Analects* is a composite text assembled by several authors over time, so passages should be attributed to the received *Analects*, not confidently to the historical Confucius. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/39170/chapter/338633165?searchresult=1)
- **Ubuntu:** Ubuntu is not one canonical doctrine or a synonym for all African ethics. Southern African Ubuntu/Botho/Hunhu, Menkiti's broader African communitarianism, Gyekye's moderate communitarianism and Metz's relational theory overlap but are not interchangeable. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/nup.70052)
- **Buddhism:** The selected passages come from the Pāli tradition and therefore cannot represent Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna or East Asian Buddhism without qualification. Early Buddhist texts passed through long oral and editorial transmission; their precise dates and verbatim attribution to the historical Buddha remain contested. [ocbs](https://ocbs.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/awynne2005wzks.pdf)
- **Mohism:** The *Mozi* is an anthology from different periods and anonymous Mohist writers, not a single book securely authored by Mo Di. [plato.stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2016/entries/mohism/texts.html)
- **Daoism:** "Daoism" includes multiple philosophical and religious traditions. The received *Zhuangzi* is composite, and scholarship now questions whether even the seven Inner Chapters originated as a coherent work by one author. [brill](https://brill.com/view/journals/tpao/96/4/article-p299_1.xml)

### Strongest criticism

The strongest objection is that role-centred reciprocity can preserve domination when the underlying roles are hierarchical. A study proposing "Confucian role ethics with non-domination" concedes that role ethics is excessively inclined to authorize hierarchy and that reciprocal duties do not by themselves make ruler–subject relations non-dominating. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8938216/)

For AI, this matters because an algorithm could faithfully reproduce the expectations of parent/child, teacher/pupil, employer/worker or state/citizen roles while leaving unequal power unquestioned. Li-Hsiang Lisa Rosenlee also traces how filial piety, patrilineal continuity and ancestor worship historically helped sustain gender oppression, while arguing that Confucian resources can nevertheless be reconstructed toward gender parity. [sunypress](https://sunypress.edu/Books/C/Confucianism-and-Women)

### Source limitation

Ubuntu originated primarily through lived practice, language and oral traditions rather than one canonical scripture. A responsible school should therefore combine a traditional maxim with modern African normative texts, while making clear that the African Charter is compatible with relational African ethics but is not itself an "Ubuntu scripture."

### Strongest criticism

Bernard Matolino and Wenceslaus Kwindingwi's **"The End of Ubuntu"** argues that post-apartheid "Ubuntu-isation" is vulnerable to elite appropriation, romantic narratives of cultural return and a gap between proclaimed communal ideals and modern social reality. Related criticism holds that Ubuntu can become vague, homogenize diverse African identities, suppress alternative interpretations and insufficiently protect women or individual rights. [scispace](https://scispace.com/papers/the-end-of-ubuntu-4i1pix8xe2)

This is the central Sophecs safeguard: "community" cannot be treated as a unified stakeholder. An Ubuntu-inspired assessment must ask who defines communal welfare, who is excluded, and whether appeals to harmony silence dissent.

### Strongest criticism

Damien Keown observes that Buddhist traditions contain extensive moral teaching but comparatively little explicit, systematic moral philosophy of the kind developed in modern Western ethics; he connects this partly to historical emphases on metaphysics, liberation and monastic practice rather than politics. For AI governance, that creates a real translation problem: compassion, non-harm and intention do not automatically specify institutional rules for data ownership, distributive fairness, audits or appeals. [link.springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40961-024-00325-4)

Charles Hallisey's **"Ethical Particularism in Theravāda Buddhism"** supplies an important response: the absence of a single abstract theory may reflect context-sensitive ethical reasoning rather than theoretical failure. Sophecs should present the institutional gap as a challenge for reconstruction, not as proof that Buddhist ethics is intellectually deficient. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781444367072.wbiee163.pub2)

### Strongest criticism

The strongest objection is that Mohist impartial welfare and objective standards are embedded within a centralized, hierarchical political order. The Mohists support a benevolent sovereign, meritocratic bureaucracy and unified ethical standards; those commitments risk turning moral agreement into enforced conformity. [plato.stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2020/entries/mohism/)

For AI, a system optimized for measurable collective benefit could suppress plural values, dissent, privacy or local relationships—especially if officials control both the standard of benefit and the evidence used to assess it. Bryan Van Norden also argues that Confucian graded concern may be better justified than Mohist impartiality, although reviewers note that this challenge must answer the Mohist argument that impartial care reduces overall social harm. [ndpr.nd](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/virtue-ethics-and-consequentialism-in-early-chinese-philosophy/)

### Classification dispute

Mohism is frequently called an early form of consequentialism, but it is not classical hedonistic utilitarianism. Its central goods include material sufficiency, social order and population or family continuity rather than a single quantity of pleasure. [plato.stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2020/entries/mohism/)

Chris Fraser therefore prefers **practice consequentialism** or **dao consequentialism**: whole social practices are evaluated by whether they benefit everyone and reduce harm. Other scholars interpret inclusive care as **rule consequentialism**, because equal moral concern can justify differentiated practices rather than requiring identical treatment of every person. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/HEMMAI)

**Authenticity flag:** Chapters 12 and 17 are Outer Chapters and should not be attributed directly to the historical Zhuang Zhou. Even the unity and authorship of the Inner Chapters, including chapter 2, are disputed. [brill](https://brill.com/view/journals/tpao/96/4/article-p299_1.xml)

### Strongest criticism

Chad Hansen's influential interpretation treats the *Zhuangzi* as endorsing perspectival or indexical relativism: classifications and judgments depend on shifting contexts and no fixed human standpoint can claim final authority. The serious objection is that this may weaken the public standards needed to condemn discriminatory algorithms, settle conflicts or impose safety constraints. [plato.stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2007/entries/zhuangzi/)

Yong Huang responds by developing a Zhuangzian "ethics of difference" that respects radically different perspectives without making moral judgment impossible. Sophecs should present the tension as **epistemic humility versus action-guiding standards**, rather than teaching that Zhuangzi simply believed "everything is equally true." [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/jaar/article/78/1/65/759171)

## Product recommendations

### School architecture

Each full school should have four separate layers:

- **Historical tradition:** texts, communities, internal development and contested dating.
- **Ethical lens:** the reconstructed principles Sophecs applies to AI.
- **Case analysis:** how the lens changes questions about data, decisions, status, care and fairness.
- **Critical challenge:** where the lens risks domination, vagueness, institutional incompleteness or cultural overgeneralization.

### Suggested AI questions

| Tradition | Lead question |
|---|---|
| Confucian role ethics | "What relationships and reciprocal obligations does this system create or damage?" |
| Ubuntu/relational ethics | "Does this system enable people to flourish through one another, and who gets excluded from 'the community'?" |
| Buddhist ethics | "What suffering, attachment or harmful intention does this system generate before, during and after use?" |
| Mohism | "What publicly testable benefit does this practice produce for everyone rather than one faction?" |
| Zhuangzi | "What perspectives has the system frozen into its categories, and how is the technology reshaping its users?" |

### Terminology safeguards

- Do not label Confucianism "collectivist"; use **relational and role-constituted**, while separately addressing hierarchy.
- Do not label Ubuntu "the African worldview"; use **a family of Southern and sub-Saharan African relational traditions**.
- Do not reduce Buddhism to mindfulness or "reducing suffering"; preserve intention, karma, liberation, wisdom and differences among Buddhist schools.
- Do not call Mohism simply "Chinese utilitarianism"; use **impartial-care or dao-consequentialist tradition**.
- Do not teach Daoism as anti-technology. Present competing readings of the "machine-heart" as rejection of machinery, dependency, instrumental consciousness or domination.
