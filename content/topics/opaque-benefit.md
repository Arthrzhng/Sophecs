title: "The opaque benefit"
motion: "An AI system that reliably reduces suffering should be deployed even where the people it decides about cannot be told why."
stances:
  stoicism: "Deploy it if the people responsible judged the evidence well; the mechanism is an external, and a sound judgement does not become unsound because the reasons cannot be passed on."
  utilitarianism: "Deploy it, because the suffering it prevents is real and an explanation was only ever a means to that end."
  virtue-ethics: "Do not deploy it, because justice to the person a decision lands on includes owing them an answer, and a practice of withholding one forms the wrong character."
  confucian: "Do not deploy it as it stands, because the person a decision lands on stands in a relationship with whoever decides, and a decision nobody can account for leaves them unable to hold the other side to what that position owes them."
  ubuntu: "Do not deploy it until the people it decides about have a part in deciding, because people who cannot ask why a decision went against them have been put outside the relationships that make them persons, whatever suffering it spares them."
  buddhist: "Deploy it only if those running it can keep checking, while it runs and after, whom it is still hurting, since reducing suffering is the point and an opacity that hides the harm it does cannot be squared with non-harm."
