read: |-
  Ubuntu is not one doctrine and not the ethics of a continent, but a
  family of Southern and sub-Saharan African relational traditions
  carried in practice and language rather than in a scripture. The Nguni
  maxim umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu is its best known compression. Sophecs
  takes Thaddeus Metz's relational reading, not Ifeanyi Menkiti's stronger
  claim that the community confers personhood once its demands are met. Kwame Gyekye's moderate version sits between them,
  insisting individuals keep their own worth and rights. Its sources are
  lived practice, language and oral tradition rather than a canon, which is
  part of why it resists reduction to a slogan.

  For AI this changes the first question. Sabelo Mhlambi argues that a
  discriminatory automated decision is not only an unfair output but a
  failure of relational personhood. Metz argues against building systems
  to maximise expected utility. Your data is rarely only yours: it
  carries your family and neighbours, people nobody asked. So the test is
  whether a system lets people flourish through one another, and who was
  left out of deciding.
gets_wrong: |-
  Bernard Matolino and Wenceslaus Kwindingwi argued in The End of Ubuntu
  that post-apartheid Ubuntu-isation is open to elite appropriation and
  rests on a romantic story about conditions that no longer exist. The
  related charge is vagueness: Ubuntu can be stretched to endorse almost
  anything, flattens very different African identities into one, and has
  protected women and dissenters poorly. That matters most where it is
  strongest, because community is not a single stakeholder. Somebody
  decides what communal welfare means and who counts as inside, and an
  appeal to harmony is a convenient way to make a dissenter the problem.
  Stoics would add that a self defined through others has handed away the
  one thing that was its own.
