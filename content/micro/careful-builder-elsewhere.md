title: Nor incite to kill
source:
  author: Dhammapada
  work: Pali canon
  section: verses 129 to 130
  translation: Bhikkhu Sujato
body: >-
  The motion is about how much blame a careful builder carries when the
  thing they built does harm. The Dhammapada's instruction is short enough
  to quote whole: treating others like oneself, neither kill nor incite to
  kill.


  The second half is the half that bears on building. Inciting is acting
  through somebody else, and the verse refuses to treat the distance as
  exculpating. The person who caused the act to happen is inside the
  prohibition with the person whose hands did it. Nothing in the line turns
  on how directly the harm was delivered.


  A model deployed at scale is distance of exactly this kind, made of
  engineering rather than of persuasion. The builder sets the objective, the
  thresholds, the data the thing learns the world from, and then is not in
  the room when any particular person is refused, or misdiagnosed, or
  flagged. The usual defence is that the harm came out of a process, and
  that a careful person cannot be blamed for a process.


  The first half of the verse is what blocks that move. Treating others like
  oneself is not a rule about outcomes to be weighed against the care taken.
  It is a demand to keep the person at the far end of the system in view,
  which is the thing the distance is good at removing.
