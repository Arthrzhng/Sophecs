title: What inciting does not cover
source:
  author: Dhammapada
  work: Pali canon
  section: verses 129 to 130
  translation: Bhikkhu Sujato
body: >-
  The motion is about how much blame a careful builder carries when what
  they built does harm. The Dhammapada's instruction is short enough to
  quote whole: treating others like oneself, neither kill nor incite to
  kill.


  The second clause looks as though it settles the question. It does not.
  Inciting is something done on purpose. You urge, or order, or pay. The
  builder in the motion did none of that: they set an objective, tested it,
  and were wrong about a case they had not foreseen. Calling that incitement
  would be a word doing work it has not earned, and the gap is where the
  argument has to happen.


  What the verse does establish is narrower. Acting through
  another person does not put you outside the prohibition. Distance from the
  act is not a defence when the distance was arranged. Whether that reaches
  from deliberate incitement to foreseeable harm, through a process you
  built and no longer control, is a question the passage does not answer. A
  Buddhist reader has to argue for the extension rather than assume it, and
  the motion's defender gets to say that intention is precisely what is
  missing.


  The first clause is doing different work. Treating others like oneself is
  not a rule about apportioning blame. It is a demand to keep the person at
  the far end of the system in view, which is what distance removes, whether
  or not anyone intended it to.
