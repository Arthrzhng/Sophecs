title: What a government needs before it is believed
source:
  author: The Analects
  work: Book 12
  section: "7, printed pp. 161 to 162"
  translation: James Legge, 1893
body: >-
  The motion puts two things on the scales: the suffering a system
  prevents, and the reason the people it decides about are never given.
  The Analects weighs a third, and treats it as the one that cannot be
  done without.


  Book 12 lists what a government requires: sufficiency of food,
  sufficiency of military equipment, and the confidence of the people in
  their ruler. The third is not a report on how the governed happen to
  feel. It sits alongside food and weapons as a requisite, so a government
  without it is not badly regarded but incomplete.


  Hold an opaque system against that and the test changes shape.
  Reliability is not confidence. A system can be right about people for
  years while the people it decides about have no standing to question it,
  and the shortfall is then not a presentational problem. It is a missing
  requisite.


  Paragraph three presses it. Asked which of the remaining two to give up,
  the Master says part with the food. From of old, death has been the lot
  of all men; but if the people have no faith in their rulers, there is no
  standing for the state. Confidence outranks survival there, which is a
  strong claim and not a decorative one.


  An explanation is one way to earn it. The passage does not say it is the
  only way, which is the uncomfortable part for both sides.
  The Confucian question is what the other grounds are, and whether anyone
  has built them.
