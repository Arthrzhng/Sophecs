title: What a government needs before it is believed
source:
  author: The Analects
  work: Book 12
  section: "7"
  translation: James Legge
body: >-
  The motion puts two things on the scales: the suffering a system prevents,
  and the explanation nobody can give for how it decides. The Analects
  weighs a third thing, and treats it as the one that cannot be done
  without.


  Book 12 lists what a government requires. Sufficiency of food,
  sufficiency of military equipment, and the confidence of the people in
  their ruler. The third item is not a report on how the governed happen to
  feel. It sits alongside food and weapons as a requisite, which is to say
  that a government without it is not a badly regarded government but an
  incomplete one.


  Hold an opaque system against that and the test changes shape. Reliability
  is not confidence. A system can be right about people for years while the
  people it decides about have no standing to question it, and on this
  reading the shortfall is not a public relations problem to be managed. It
  is a missing requisite.


  An explanation is one way to earn confidence. The passage does not say it
  is the only way, which is the uncomfortable part for both sides of the
  motion. Institutions people cannot audit are trusted all the time, on
  other grounds. The Confucian question is what those other grounds are
  here, and whether anyone has built them.
