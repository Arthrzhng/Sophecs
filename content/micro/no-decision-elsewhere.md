title: If the names are not correct
source:
  author: The Analects
  work: Book 13
  section: "3"
  translation: James Legge
body: >-
  The motion is a claim about a word. It says that calling what a language
  model does a decision is a mistake. Book 13 of the Analects is about
  exactly that kind of mistake, and about why it is not merely verbal.


  If names be not correct, the passage says, language is not in accordance
  with the truth of things, and if language is not in accordance with the
  truth of things, affairs cannot be carried on to success. The order
  matters. A wrong name is not the end of the problem. It is the start of a
  chain that ends somewhere practical, in work that fails and in people
  treated as the wrong name implies.


  The words around these systems are doing a great deal of unearned work.
  Intelligent. Autonomous. Fair. Explainable. Each one imports a claim that
  the thing underneath has not established, and each one travels: into a
  procurement document, into a line of policy, into the sentence an official
  says to someone whose application was refused.


  So the rectification reading does not ask whether a model really decides,
  in the way a philosopher of mind would ask it. It asks what the word
  decides for us once we have let it stand, and who ends up carrying the
  consequence of a name nobody corrected.
