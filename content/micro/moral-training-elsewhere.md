title: The mirror given to Rahula
source:
  author: Majjhima Nikaya
  work: Ambalatthika-Rahulovada Sutta
  section: "61"
  translation: Lord Chalmers, 1926
body: >-
  The motion asks whether training a model on human feedback can make it
  good the way habit makes a person good. Discourse 61 is itself a training
  instruction, which makes it a useful thing to hold the comparison against.


  The method it gives is reflection, run three times over one act. Before
  you do the thing, you reflect on whether it would conduce to your harm, or
  to another's, or to both. You reflect again while you are doing it. You
  reflect once more when it is done, and if harm has come of it you stop and
  you say so.


  Notice what is being trained. Not the act, and not a disposition to
  produce the act. What is being trained is the looking: a habit of turning
  attention onto your own conduct and asking a specific question about it.
  The discourse treats that inward turn as the thing worth installing,
  because it is what lets a person handle a case nobody has coached them
  through.


  Reward shaping can install the act. It is much less clear that it installs
  the looking, and on this reading the difference is not a technicality. A
  system that behaves well and cannot examine itself has been given the
  output of the training without the part the training was for.
