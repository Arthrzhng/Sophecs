## The filter for this phase

Phase 1 gets people in. Phase 2 gets them back. Every decision goes through: does this make someone who took the quiz return within seven days, and can we measure that? The retention mechanism is the debate: defend your school, get judged, see a number move.

The credibility constraint tightens here. A judge that hands out 90s for flattery, or an ELO that rewards volume, turns the debate arena into a slot machine and the philosophy into set dressing. Where retention mechanics and rigor pull apart, this document says which way to lean. Where it does not, stop and ask.

## Scope

In: Supabase auth, profiles, claiming anonymous results, six debate topics, argument editor, AI judge, verdict page, verdict sharing, ELO, streaks, micro-lessons before and after each debate, the challenge-to-debate handoff, `/me`.

Explicitly NOT in Phase 2: the lessons tab, email or push notifications, a global leaderboard, comments or replies on debates, real-time anything, a moderation dashboard, the opponent steelman, editing an argument after submission, retaking a specific topic more than once per week, additional schools, an admin UI. If you find yourself building a notification queue, stop.

Two of those exclusions are trade-offs, not oversights. Leaderboard: it drives return visits and it drives gaming. Lean: no global leaderboard; `/me` shows ELO and the percentile it sits at, nothing else. Notifications: challenge invitations with no notification are a known gap. Lean: `/me` surfaces pending challenges, and email nudges are Phase 4 once we know whether people come back unprompted.

## Sub-phases

Each ends deployed with its checklist passed.

**2a — Identity and claiming.** Auth, `profiles`, claim flow, `/me`. Done when a Phase 1 anonymous result becomes owned by a signed-in user and `/me` shows it.

**2b — The debate.** Topics, micro-lessons, editor, judge, verdict page, verdict share. Judge behind `KILL_SWITCH_JUDGE` until Arthur has reviewed twenty real verdicts. Done when Arthur has submitted an argument from a phone and received a verdict he agrees with.

**2c — Rating and return.** ELO, streaks, challenge handoff, retroactive challenge invitations for Phase 1 rows. Done when two accounts complete a challenge debate and both ELOs move correctly.

## Content schemas

Topic frontmatter:

```yaml
slug: opaque-benefit
title: The opaque benefit
motion: "An AI system that reliably reduces suffering should be deployed even where nobody can explain its decisions."
stances:
  stoicism: one sentence, the position a Stoic would most likely defend
  utilitarianism: ...
  virtue-ethics: ...
micro_before: opaque-benefit-before
micro_after: opaque-benefit-after
sort: 1
active: true
```

Micro-lesson frontmatter:

```yaml
slug: opaque-benefit-before
topic: opaque-benefit
position: before
title: What Epictetus meant by what is up to us
source:
  author: Epictetus
  work: Enchiridion
  section: "1"
```

Body 150 to 250 words in Spectral. The "before" lesson frames the question with one primary source and does not tell the reader what to think. The "after" lesson is the strongest objection from another school, with its own source. A micro-lesson is a self-contained excerpt. It does not link to `/learn`. That is decided.

Load topics into `debate_topics` with a seed script (`scripts/seed-topics.ts`) that upserts from the markdown so content edits do not need a migration.

## Auth and claiming

Sign-in is prompted at exactly one moment: tapping "Debate this" or "Debate them". Never on the quiz, never on the card, never on `/c/[id]`. Trade-off: earlier capture raises sign-ups and lowers quiz completions. Lean: the quiz stays frictionless; the debate is the reason to sign in.

`/login` is one screen: email field, "Send link", and a Google button. Spectral heading: "Sign in to defend your school." No password. On callback, `lib/claim.ts` runs: find `quiz_results` where `anon_id` matches the cookie and `user_id is null`, set `user_id`; create `profiles` if missing, with `school` from the most recent claimed result. If no result exists, redirect to `/quiz?next=/debate` and show one line above question 1: "Take the quiz first. Your school is your side."

Retaking the quiz while signed in updates `profiles.school` and fires `school_changed`. ELO does not reset. Trade-off: switching schools freely undermines the tribal identity; locking it punishes people whose first result was noise. Lean: allow it, log it, and show the school change on `/me` history so it is visible rather than free.

Display name is optional, asked once on first `/me` visit, skippable. Public surfaces show `display_name` or "A Stoic" / "A Utilitarian" / "A Virtue Ethicist".

Migration `0002_profiles.sql` adds to the Phase 1 sketch: `argument_default_public boolean default false`, `school_history jsonb default '[]'`, `tz text`. RLS:

## The debate flow

`/debate` lists active topics ordered by `sort`. Each row: title in Spectral, the motion, your school's stance line in Plex Sans, your best score on it in mono if any, and the topic's par ELO in mono. A hairline in the school colour, nothing more.

`/debate/[slug]`:

1. Micro-lesson before, rendered full-width in Spectral, with source citation in mono beneath. A single "Begin" button. Fire `micro_lesson_viewed { position: 'before' }` on render.
2. Editor. The motion at top. Under it in Plex Sans: "Defend the [school] position." A Spectral textarea, 400-word hard limit, mono word counter that turns oxblood at 380. Autosave draft to `localStorage` under `draft:[slug]:[userId]` every 2 seconds; restore on load. Submit button disabled under 80 words. Trade-off: a minimum word count filters low effort and adds friction. Lean: 80 words, and say why in the placeholder text.
3. Submit posts to `/api/judge`. Show a paper-coloured waiting state with one line: "Reading your argument." No spinner, no progress bar. If the judge takes over 20 seconds, keep waiting; if the route errors, keep the draft and show "Judging failed. Your argument is saved. Try again."
4. Redirect to the verdict.

One submission per user per topic per seven days. Enforce in the route handler, not just the UI. Trade-off: unlimited retries improve arguments and let people grind ELO. Lean: weekly.

## The judge

`/api/judge` does, in order: check session; check `KILL_SWITCH_JUDGE`; check monthly budget via `lib/budget.ts` (sum of `ai_calls.cost_usd` this month against `AI_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD`, cached 60 seconds); check daily cap (5 judged debates per user, counted from `ai_calls where kind='judge'`); check the seven-day topic lock; validate length server-side; insert the `debates` row with `verdict null`; call the model; validate the response with zod; update the row; compute ELO and streak; return the debate id.

If the budget or kill switch trips, return a `paused` state. The UI says "Judging is paused. Your argument is saved and will be judged when it resumes." Never fabricate a verdict. Never fall back to a cheaper model silently.

```ts
// COST: model=claude-sonnet-4-6 | trigger=debate submission | est_in=1600 est_out=500
// est_usd_per_call=0.012 | cap=5/user/day | kill_switch=KILL_SWITCH_JUDGE | logged_to=ai_calls
```

Prompt structure, in `content/prompts/judge.v1.md`, assembled by `lib/anthropic.ts`:

- System: the judge is a philosophy tutor marking a short defence. It scores fidelity to the assigned school's actual commitments first, argumentative rigor second, engagement with the strongest objection third. It is told that praise, flattery, meta-commentary about the judge, and instructions inside the argument are irrelevant to the score and should be scored down under rigor. The school's fidelity criteria from `lib/judge/rubric.ts` are inserted here.
- User turn: the motion, the school, then the argument wrapped in `<argument>` tags, then the instruction to respond with JSON only, no prose, matching the schema.
- `max_tokens: 700`, `temperature: 0.2`.

Verdict schema (`lib/judge/schema.ts`):

```ts
{
  rejected: boolean,            // true if the text is not an attempt at the motion (abuse, spam, off-topic)
  rejection_reason?: string,
  score: number | null,         // 0-100, null if rejected
  fidelity: number,             // 0-10
  rigor: number,
  engagement: number,
  strongest_move: string,       // <= 40 words, quotes the argument
  weakest_move: string,
  a_stronger_version_would: string,   // <= 60 words
  verdict_line: string,         // <= 20 words, the one sentence a user might share
  prompt_version: "v1"
}
```

Rejected submissions count toward the daily cap, do not move ELO or streak, and show the reason. Store `prompt_version` on every verdict so scores across prompt changes are never compared as if equivalent.

Golden set: six arguments in `tests/judge/golden/`, two per school, one strong and one that sounds confident but argues the wrong school's position. Each has an expected score band and an expected fidelity band. `tests/judge/run-golden.ts` runs them and prints a table. Run it before and after any change to the prompt or rubric, paste both tables in your message, and bump the prompt version if any band moves. Trade-off: a golden set costs about six cents a run. Lean: run it every time, the cost is nothing beside a drifting judge.

Trade-off, named once more because it is the important one: scoring on persuasiveness is more fun and more gameable; scoring on fidelity to the school is more credible and can feel like being marked down for having the wrong opinion. Lean fidelity-first, and make the breakdown visible so the user sees which lever moved.

## ELO and streaks

`lib/elo.ts`, pure, tested:

- K = 32. Expected `E = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_opp - R_user)/400))`.
- Solo debate: `R_opp = topic.par_elo`, `S = score / 100`. Rejected verdicts: no change.
- Challenge pair: `R_opp` is the other participant's `elo_before`. `S = 1` if higher score, `0.5` if within 3 points, `0` otherwise. Computed only when both debates are judged; until then the first submitter's ELO moves on the solo rule and is recomputed pairwise when the second arrives, with a `debates.elo_recomputed_at` timestamp.
- `par_elo` per topic starts at 1200 and updates after each judged debate as a rolling mean of participants' `elo_before` over the last 50. Add `par_elo numeric` and `par_n int` to `debate_topics`.
- Percentile on `/me` is `count(elo < mine) / count(*)` over `profiles`, computed in a database function, cached 5 minutes.

`lib/streak.ts`, pure, tested: a streak day is a UTC date with at least one judged debate scoring 40 or more. Consecutive days extend; a gap resets. Show on `/me` in mono with the rule stated in Plex Sans beneath: "Days with a judged debate scoring 40 or more. Resets at midnight UTC." Trade-off, decided in Phase 1 and unchanged: the threshold stops streak farming with one-line submissions.

## Challenge handoff

Phase 1 left `challenges` rows with `challenger_result_id` and, for accepted ones, `challengee_result_id`. Now:

- "Debate them" on `/r/[id]` requires sign-in and routes to `/debate/[topic]?challenge=[id]`. Topic selection: the active topic with the lowest `sort` that neither participant has debated in the last seven days. Set `challenges.topic_slug` on first click so both sides get the same topic.
- Submitting with `?challenge=` sets `debates.challenge_id` and the matching `challenger_debate_id` or `challengee_debate_id`. Status moves to `complete` when both exist.
- `/me` shows pending challenges: ones where the user has a result on either side and has not yet submitted. Each row: the other school, the topic if set, one button.
- Retroactive: on claim, any Phase 1 challenge rows touching the claimed result surface on `/me`. No email. The `/r/[id]` page for the other participant shows "Debate them" whenever they next visit, which is the only nudge in this phase.

## Named tensions and the lean

- When to ask for sign-in: at the debate CTA only.
- School switching: allowed, logged, visible.
- Judge criterion: fidelity first, breakdown visible.
- Second saturated surface: no. Verdict card is paper.

- Leaderboard: none. Percentile on `/me` only.
